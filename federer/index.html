Data

  • TV recordings of 45 of his US Open matches, 2003–2019, some only highlights. We only include live play from the main camera angle, in which Federer is standing on the near end.
  • His joints (head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles and feet) were tracked in every frame.
  • Every hit was labelled automatically as a serve, forehand or backhand.
  • Distances are in centimetres, from the court's known size.

Syllables

  • His movement was split into short, repeated movements ("syllables") with keypoint-MoSeq, from his joints alone. It found 30; a typical one lasts 0.5 s.
  • The serve was over-segmented into several syllables in a row, so they were merged into one, Serve.
  • On the map, each dot is one occurrence of a syllable, and similar movements sit close together (UMAP).
  • The names of each syllable were given by hand after watching each syllable's typical clips.

Then vs Now

  • His 274 forehands and 375 serves, typical strokes only: none hit on the run or far from the baseline. His 166 backhands were tested the same way and nothing changed reliably.
  • Camera zoom, picture quality, where he stood and how he moved were regressed out before comparing.
  • Each match gives one number, his average. 2017–19 is compared with 2003–07 by Welch's t-test, corrected (Holm) for the number of measures. A change is shown when the corrected p is below 0.05 and it is at least 0.4 of the spread between his strokes.
  • Charts: each dot is an era's average with its 95% interval; stars mark the corrected p (* below 0.05, ** 0.01, *** 0.001). The grey line is his opponents in the same matches.
